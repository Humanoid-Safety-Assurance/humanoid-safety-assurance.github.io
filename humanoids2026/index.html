01 The motivation

Beyond capability.
Toward justified confidence.

Demonstrating a humanoid’s capabilities is only one part of understanding whether it can operate safely around people. Human environments demand reliable behavior under uncertainty, awareness of physical and social risks, and evidence that a system’s safeguards hold beyond a successful demonstration.

This workshop brings together perspectives from humanoid robotics, safety, verification, human–robot interaction, and evaluation to explore how that evidence can be built, tested, and communicated.

?

The central technical question

How can we turn humanoid capability into credible, measurable assurance for safe and trustworthy operation in human environments?

Define the risks.Build the evidence.Justify the trust.

02 Topics of interest

The foundations of assurance.

Connecting methods, systems, and evidence across the humanoid research community.

01

Safety-aware planning & control

Risk-sensitive decisions, physical constraints, contact-aware control, and runtime safety mechanisms.

02

Verification, validation & assurance

Formal methods, safety cases, system-level testing, and evidence for trustworthy behavior.

03

Human–humanoid interaction

Safe physical collaboration, predictable behavior, human factors, and appropriate trust.

04

Robustness & failure recovery

Uncertainty, distribution shifts, out-of-distribution detection, and recovery from failures.

05

Benchmarks & evaluation

Simulation, real-world testing, meaningful metrics, and reproducible assessment of safety.

06

Responsible deployment

Operational boundaries, ongoing monitoring, incident learning, and lifecycle assurance.

03 Call for papers

Help shape the assurance agenda.

We invite contributions that connect humanoid capabilities with evidence of safety, reliability, and trustworthiness in human environments.

Relevant work may address technical methods, system design, evaluation practices, or open research challenges across the workshop topics. We are particularly interested in the connection between proposed safeguards and the evidence used to assess them.
