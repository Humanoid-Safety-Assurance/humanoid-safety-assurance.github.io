01 The motivation

Assurance across the humanoid system.

Humanoid safety requires more than safe AI models or safe robot control considered in isolation. Learning-enabled humanoids connect perception, reasoning, planning, whole-body action, physical interaction, runtime supervision, and recovery.

Errors or uncertainty at one layer can propagate into unsafe contact, balance loss, object dropping, or inappropriate whole-body actions around people. This workshop examines how safeguards work across these layers and what evidence can demonstrate their effectiveness under uncertainty and failure.

?

Central technical questions

How can humanoid systems perceive, reason, plan, act, and recover safely under uncertainty and failure, and how can these safety mechanisms be effectively evaluated?

What evidence is sufficient to trust a learning-enabled humanoid operating physically around people?

02 Topics of interest

The foundations of assurance.

Safeguards and evidence across learning, whole-body behavior, and physical interaction.

01

Safety-aware planning & control

Linking reasoning and task plans to whole-body constraints, balance, and contact-aware action.

02

Verification, validation & assurance

Testing assumptions across learned components, controllers, and runtime safeguards; building system-level evidence.

03

Human–humanoid interaction

Safe contact, object handling, and predictable whole-body behavior around people.

04

Runtime supervision & recovery

Detecting uncertainty and failures, supervising learned actions, and recovering from unsafe states.

05

Benchmarks & evaluation

Evaluating error propagation, physical consequences, and safeguard effectiveness in simulation and real-world tests.

06

Responsible deployment

Defining operational boundaries and connecting monitoring and incident evidence to lifecycle assurance.

Call for Papers & Demos

Paper submission ↓ Call for demos ↓ Important dates ↓

We invite papers and demos addressing safety and assurance for humanoid systems operating in human environments.

We welcome two types of paper submissions:

Short / Position Papers — up to 4 pages

Early-stage research, position papers, preliminary results, failure analyses, benchmark proposals, safety architectures, and emerging research directions.

Research Papers — 4–8 pages recommended

Substantially developed research contributions, including methods, system studies, experiments, benchmarks, and safety evaluation studies.

Paper submission information

Paper Submit!

Paper submissions open October 2, 2026.

Format
Paper submissions should use the IEEE standard double-column conference format (4–8 pages recommended). One additional page may be used exclusively for references. Appendices are not considered as part of the review submission.
Review process
Papers will undergo single-blind review. Authors do not need to anonymize their submissions.
Presentation
Accepted papers will be presented as posters. Selected papers will additionally be invited for short spotlight presentations.
Publication policy
No formal proceedings (we are a non-archival workshop). Authors are welcome to post their papers on arXiv.

Call for Demos

Demo Submit!

Paper and demo submissions use the same OpenReview portal.

We welcome demos addressing safety and assurance for humanoid systems, using simulation, portable equipment, or physical robots.

Examples include safe physical interaction, whole-body behavior, runtime supervision and recovery, and evaluation of safety mechanisms under uncertainty and failure.

File format
Any file format is accepted.
Title / heading
The title or heading of your submitted document must clearly include the word Demo.
Demo materials
Include a demo video or animation and a brief description of the demo's functionality. Clearly state whether the demo uses simulation, portable equipment, or a physical robot.
Equipment
Applicants are generally expected to bring their own demo equipment. Please specify any additional requirements in your submission.
Presentation arrangements
Details of the demo presentation format, schedule, and on-site setup arrangements will be announced.
